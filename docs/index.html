Nexus is a pair of Klipper controller boards for a hotend changer with up to eight tools, designed with the YUDX tool changer in mind. One board runs the heaters, thermistors and filament sensors, the other runs the dock fans.

Built around YUDX

Electronics for a hotend changer

YUDX is an open-source tool changer for the Voron 2.4 and Trident. Every tool head has its own heater and thermistor, so parked tools can stay hot. That is fast, but it means up to eight heaters, eight thermistors and eight filament paths that all need to be controlled and watched.

Nexus puts all of that on two purpose-built boards. Klipper supports multiple MCUs natively, so each board is simply another MCU in your config: no custom protocol, no extra daemon.

Independent project. Nexus is not affiliated with or endorsed by the YUDX authors. YUDX's own G-code targets RepRapFirmware; Nexus is Klipper-only.
The hardware

Two boards, one design language

Same MCU, same firmware flow, same diagnostic approach. Parts are shared between the boards wherever possible and everything is in stock at LCSC.

Tools

Nexus Tools Board

Mounted close to where the umbilicals start. One cable runs from here to each tool head.

  • 8 heater channels, up to 70 W each, all eight at once from a cold start
  • 8 thermistor inputs, NTC 100K or PT1000 per channel, 0.1 % pull-ups
  • 8 filament ports, each with a runout and a motion input for clog detection
  • Hardware cutoff of the heater rail with a self-test at every startup
  • Diagnostic LED per rail and per heater channel
  • WS2812 port, USB-C and CAN with USB-CAN bridge, 24 V in via XT60
Docks

Nexus Docks Board

Mounted at the docks, where the parked tools need their cooling.

  • 9 fan outputs, up to 1 A per fan and 3 A in total
  • Fan voltage 5, 12 or 24 V, selectable per board. Nothing is selected by default, so a 5 V fan never gets 24 V by accident
  • Diagnostic LEDs for every rail, USB and MCU
  • WS2812 port, USB-C and CAN
  • Own 24 V input via XT30, with reverse polarity protection
Why Nexus

Built to make faults visible

Most printer boards only show whether they have power. Nexus shows where things go wrong, and puts safety in the hardware instead of only in the firmware.

01 / CUTOFF

Hardware heater cutoff

The heater rail is switched by Klipper and off by default. If the firmware stops, resets or hangs, the heaters lose power — even if a heater MOSFET has failed short.

02 / SELF-TEST

Proven at every start

A voltage measurement on the heater rail checks at every startup that the cutoff actually switches.

03 / LEDS

See what the MOSFET does

Each heater channel has an LED that shows what the MOSFET is really doing, not what the software thinks it is doing.

04 / CLOGS

Clog detection per tool

Runout and motion input on every filament port. Use a simple D2F switch, or a motion sensor that notices a clogged nozzle or a slipping extruder.

05 / CONFIG

Simple Klipper setup

Connect over USB-C or CAN, and use the ready-made config and macros for YUDX: one block per tool, and Klipper sees eight full hotends. Disable a tool by commenting out its block.

06 / OPEN

Fully open source

KiCad files, BOM with LCSC numbers, production files for JLCPCB and the Klipper config are all in the repository.

Protection

Two protection layers in the board

On top of a fuse per heater channel, an input that withstands reverse polarity and voltage spikes, and ESD protection on every external signal connector.

LAYER 1

Klipper

verify_heater, temperature limits and communication monitoring.

LAYER 2

Hardware cutoff

Two 60 V MOSFETs in the heater rail supply, off by default. On shutdown, reset or hung firmware the heater rail drops out. Checked by a self-test at every startup.

Want an extra passive layer? Use PTC heaters (self-limiting) or fit a thermal fuse on the heatsink or tool holder. A thermal fuse on the heater block itself does not work: those trip around 240 °C, at or below print temperature.

⚠ Read this before you build or use a board

This is a hobby project and a work in progress. The boards are not built, not tested, not certified and not CE marked. They drive heaters: a mistake in the design, assembly, wiring or configuration can cause a fire.

Design limits: 24 V DC only · max. 70 W per heater channel · max. 8 channels · NTC 100K or PT1000 only (no thermocouples or PT100) · Klipper only, with the included safety macros.

Mandatory:

  1. Klipper's verify_heater active, with sensible max_temp values.
  2. Keep the cutoff startup self-test enabled (NEXUS_SELFTEST, runs at every Klipper start). Do not print if it fails; call NEXUS_PRINT_START in your PRINT_START so a print cannot start without it.
  3. Run the commissioning macro NEXUS_COMMISSION after every (re)wiring: it checks that each heater heats the correct thermistor.
  4. Never print unattended until the whole setup has proven itself over multiple prints.
  5. One PSU, or PSUs with a common negative.
  6. Check the power cable polarity before you plug in: on the board connector (XT60 on the Tools Board, XT30 on the Docks Board) + is pin 2, marked + on the silkscreen. Wire your plug to match.

The design is provided "as is", without warranty of any kind. You build and use it entirely at your own risk. See the disclaimers in the licenses.

What you need

  • A 24 V power supply. Budget ≥ 600 W for both boards with eight 70 W heaters; less with lighter heaters or fewer tools.
  • A Klipper host (e.g. a Raspberry Pi) with Mainsail, Fluidd or similar.
  • Optional: a CAN adapter, or use the Tools Board itself as a USB-CAN bridge.

How to order

JLCPCB has no link that opens a ready-made project for someone else, so ordering takes a few uploads. Everything is prepared in fab/rev-A, with the exact settings in its README.

  1. On jlcpcb.com, upload <board>-gerbers.zip. Choose 4 layers, stackup JLC04161H-7628, 1 oz outer copper.
  2. Turn on PCB assembly, top side, and upload <board>-bom-jlc.csv and <board>-cpl-jlc.csv. All parts should be found and ticked.
  3. In the placement preview, check pin 1 of the ICs, diodes and the USB-C connector against the checklist.
  4. Order the parts in <board>-handsolder.csv from LCSC and solder them yourself (XT60/XT30, screw terminals, JST-XH, Micro-Fit, pin headers, fuses, bulk capacitors).
  5. Read the safety section before the first power-up, and check the polarity of your power cable.
